**A colour palette.** Six muted hues — red, amber, green, blue, violet, slate —
plus none, chosen as swatches in the inspector or typed after the text as
`rect: Blocked (red)`. One colour at a time. They are deliberately the desaturated
end of each hue: enough to say "this is the destructive path" or "this bit is
still open", not enough to start a conversation about brand colour. Each hue is
three tokens (line, ink, fill) and a colour modifier does nothing but rebind the
tokens every widget already draws from, so it works on a button, a shape, a table
and a sticky note without a single per-widget rule.

**Dropped elements are the size they should be.** Dropping a modal divided its
action row equally between Cancel and Delete — two 223px buttons in a 488px row.
A widget with a natural size now keeps it, a `(right)` or `(center)` row puts its
contents where a real dialog puts them, and a container sized by its contents
closes under them instead of leaving dead space. Eleven widget defaults were also
measured and corrected: a one-line paragraph asked for an 80px box and needed 19.

**Shapes drop empty.** `rect` and `circle` used to arrive with sample text inside,
so the first thing anyone did with a rectangle was delete it. Fill, outline and
dashed are still there as `(fill)` and `(dashed)`, or as chips in the inspector.

**Modifiers apply on every widget.** Forty of the eighty-two used the text field
raw, so anything in brackets was printed rather than applied — `note: Ask legal
(blue)` put "(blue)" on the sticky. Modifiers are now stripped and applied in one
place, which fixes all forty and means a widget added later cannot bring it back.
